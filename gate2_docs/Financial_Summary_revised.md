**Overview**
Our lean plan is modeled on Beli. Fixed costs are about $10,500 per quarter in Years 1 and 2, revenue begins in Year 2, and Year 5 is our first profitable year, with about $355,500 in revenue and $72,100 in net income. Cumulative losses are deepest in Year 4, at about $191,800, so we plan for $240,000: about $74,000 from grants and pitch competitions for Years 1 and 2, with the founders covering any shortfall, and about $166,000 from one angel round at the end of Year 2, once restaurants are paying.

**Key assumptions**
User growth is modeled at one quarter of Beli's size at the same age. Beli grew from about 200,000 to 1.1 million monthly users between early 2024 and October 2025 (Sensor Tower via the Boston Globe, Nov 2025). Following Beli, 80% of new users arrive through a friend (Wall Street Journal, May 2025), and the plan includes no paid acquisition. An active user stays 18 months, the midpoint of the 12 to 24 month range from class, so about 5.6% leave each month. Each user logs four restaurant visits a month, and one in 100 visits is a diner we sent to a paying restaurant, a team estimate and the first assumption we will test.
**Unit economics**
A restaurant pays about $0.90 for each diner we send whose receipt confirms the visit. The model uses $0.87, the amount Ibotta earns per verified redemption. That is below the $1.00 to $1.50 OpenTable charges for each seated diner (OpenTable pricing via Eat App, 2026), since a new platform needs restaurants to try it first. The restaurant line also includes referral fees from booking partners for each table we send. Chains pay $6,000 per area per year, a quarter of what Placer.ai's average customer pays. We reach 3, 8 and 18 areas by the end of Years 3 to 5; because areas are added during the year, revenue reflects an average of 1.5, 5.5 and 13 areas. About 0.3% of average active users choose the optional $5-per-month membership.
From Year 3, a user is worth about $1.22 after cost of goods sold over an 18-month lifetime. Marketing costs $0.42 per new user in Year 1 and $0.07 in Year 3, so in Year 3 lifetime value is about 18 times the cost to acquire a user. In Year 2 the ratio rises each quarter, from about 2.7 to 5.7 times, and passes the three-times guideline from class in the second quarter of revenue. All of these figures count cash costs only and exclude founder time. Cost of goods sold covers reading receipts ($0.01 each on AWS, with one in ten sent to the cloud), hosting ($25 per month on Supabase) and card processing (2.9% plus $0.30 per charge).
**Fixed expenses**
Founders take no salary and handle restaurant and chain sales. Research and development is a contract developer at $2,500 per month (a team estimate). From Year 3 the developer is joined by co-op students, about one for every 140,000 average active users (1, 2 and 3 in Years 3 to 5), at about $52,000 per seat. Sales and marketing is $500 per month for campus marketing, far below the 30 to 55% of revenue typical for consumer companies, since most new users should come through friends. General and administrative costs (legal, accounting, insurance) are $500 per month in Years 1 and 2, rising to $2,000 to $4,000 per month in Years 3 to 5 as chain contracts and co-op payroll add work. A full-time salaried hire would come only once gross profit covers the salary, which the model first reaches at the end of Year 5, so none appears in the plan.
**Key performance metrics**
Active users grow from 12,000 in Year 1 to about 540,000 in Year 5, 2.8% of US college students (NCES). Chain areas reach 3, 8 and 18 in Years 3 to 5, and recurring revenue (memberships and chains) is about 41% of Year 5 revenue.
